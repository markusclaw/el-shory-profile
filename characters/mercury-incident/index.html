MERCURY MONDAY

The Incident of Late 1980s | Exact Date Disputed

Overview

According to partially documented local accounts, an industrial incident involving mercury exposure occurred at an unnamed thermostat manufacturing facility somewhere within the border region industrial zone.

The exact year remains disputed. Most witnesses claim 1987. Others insist "earlier." All details that follow are drawn from unreliable testimony, rumor, and oral tradition.

The Facility

Multiple witnesses describe an industrial thermostat manufacturing plant that:

  • No longer appears on official maps
  • Specialized in mercury tilt relays and climate-control components
  • Was described as "hotter than outside and smelling like batteries"
  • Employed an unknown number of workers
One witness claimed workers routinely handled broken containment bulbs containing liquid mercury during unsupervised shifts.

The Mercury Source

Persistent rumors suggest the mercury originated from:

  • Eastern European industrial surplus channels
  • Ukrainian decommissioned Cold War inventory
  • Mislabeled wartime material stock

Multiple witnesses reference:

  • Military-style crates with faded foreign markings
  • Missing customs documentation
  • Unlabeled containers stored improperly
No records confirm these claims. No agency has acknowledged investigating the import chain.

Documented Exposure Conditions

According to witness accounts, workplace safety protocols were:

  • Nonexistent or ignored
  • Enforcement was minimal
  • Noncompliance violations were filed but unaddressed
"You could see silver beads rolling on the floor."
— Anonymous witness testimony

The Tuna Incident

A recurring local rumor, impossible to verify:

Workers routinely consumed canned tuna sandwiches during overnight shifts while simultaneously handling mercury-contaminated materials.

Some residents speculate this combination:

  • Accelerated neurological symptoms
  • Worsened absorption rates
  • Contributed to behavioral irregularities
Medical basis for this claim remains highly questionable. The story persists in local folklore regardless.

El Shory Connection

Unverified reports place a young Jorge "El Shory" Shortinez Hernández-Reyes at or near the facility during the incident period.

Accounts conflict:

  • Some claim he worked shipping for the facility
  • Others insist he handled incoming crates
  • Still others say he cleaned damaged relay components
  • A minority claim he was never officially employed there
No documentation confirms any of these claims. Shory himself has never provided a detailed account.

Aftermath & Cover-Up

Following the incident:

  • The facility reportedly shut down
  • Records disappeared
  • Environmental cleanup was never completed
  • No agency has acknowledged contamination

Locals claim:

  • Soil conditions changed
  • Livestock became sick
  • Agricultural yields declined for years
  • Corn production never fully recovered
These claims have never been scientifically documented or officially investigated.

Reported Health Effects

Within the region, residents commonly attribute the following conditions to "Mercury Monday":

  • Thyroid dysfunction
  • Voice damage and hoarseness
  • Chronic inflammation
  • Memory issues
  • Paranoia and conspiratorial thinking
  • Chronic fatigue
  • "Looking permanently tired"
No causal link has ever been established between the incident and these conditions. Attribution remains speculative and folkloric.

Cultural Impact

Within the region, "Mercury Monday" became:

  • Urban legend
  • Conspiracy theory
  • Workplace cautionary tale
  • Explanation for nearly everything unusual

Common phrases that emerged:

"Eso viene desde el mercurio."
"Desde ese día nada crece igual."

The incident became the foundational mythology for an entire region's sense of contamination and suspicion.

Current Status

The original facility site is believed abandoned.

Satellite imagery allegedly shows:

  • Collapsed structures
  • Evidence of illegal dumping
  • Intermittent vehicle activity at night
Several locals claim El Shory still visits the area. Usually at night. Nobody has confirmed following him there.

Conclusion

The Mercury Incident remains:

  • Partially documented
  • Heavily disputed
  • Impossible to verify
  • Deeply believed

What is certain: the story shapes how the region understands itself. Whether the incident actually occurred is almost irrelevant. The mythology is real.
